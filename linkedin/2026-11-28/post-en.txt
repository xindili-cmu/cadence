Stroke Upper Limb Exoskeletons & MS VR/Aerobic Exercise: Today's Neuro Rehab Highlights

6 updates today across 5 specialties. 5 worth your time 👇

1️⃣ [Neuro] Efficacy of exoskeleton rehabilitation robots for upper limb function recovery in stroke patients: a systematic review and meta-analysis
For stroke patients with upper limb motor impairment, exoskeleton-assisted training is more effective than conventional rehabilitation, especially on core motor function assessments like FMA-UE.
— Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation

2️⃣ [Neuro] Augmenting Conventional Rehabilitation with Virtual Reality or Aerobic Exercise in Multiple Sclerosis: A Three-Arm Parallel-Group Superiority Randomized Controlled Trial.
This RCT confirms that adding Virtual Reality (VR) or Aerobic Exercise (AE) to conventional rehabilitation for Multiple Sclerosis (MS) patients is equally effective in improving functional performance, reducing fatigue, and enhancing cognition as conventional rehabilitation alone.
— NeuroRehabilitation

3️⃣ [Geriatric] Relationship between physical activity and stem cell production: A systematic review
This systematic review confirms the biological basis of exercise therapy across different age groups, including older adults: regular endurance or resistance training effectively promotes stem cell production and mobilization, supporting tissue repair.
— South African Journal of Physiotherapy

4️⃣ [Orthopedic] Video-based assessment of functional movement quality in female adults with surgical and non-surgical scoliosis and controls: a rater-blinded observational study.
This observational study suggests that the impact of long spinal fusion on some movement tasks may only become apparent at later follow-ups.
— BMC musculoskeletal disorders

5️⃣ [Sports] Isometric muscle strength and inter-limb differences in relation to countermovement jump performance during the return-to-sport phase after ACL reconstruction: a cross-sectional study
Combined assessment of unilateral isometric strength and bilateral countermovement jump (CMJ) is valuable during the return-to-sport (RTS) phase after ACL reconstruction (ACLR).
— BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders
