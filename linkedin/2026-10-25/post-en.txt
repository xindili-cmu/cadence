Wobble Board & HIIT: New Evidence in Diabetic Neuropathy and Cardiopulmonary Function

8 updates today across 5 specialties. 5 worth your time 👇

1️⃣ [Neuro] Effectiveness of Wobble Board and Swiss Ball exercises on balance, mobility and functional independence in patients with diabetic peripheral neuropathy
Both Swiss Ball and Wobble Board exercises combined with conventional therapy improved balance, mobility, and functional independence in patients with diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN), but the Swiss Ball group showed statistically greater improvements.
— Bulletin of Faculty of Physical Therapy

2️⃣ [Cardiopulmonary] Divergent functional responses to hyperoxic versus hypoxic high-intensity interval training: a randomized exploratory active-comparator study
This exploratory randomized controlled study suggests that Tabata-format high-intensity interval training (HIIT) in hyperoxia leads to broader aerobic functional improvements and lower post-exercise peak lactate, while hypoxia yielded smaller gains.
— BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation

3️⃣ [Orthopedic] Implementation, feasibility and optimization of a web- and mobile-supported semi-automated shoulder rehabilitation system (WMSSR): a prospective consecutive interventional cohort study.
This cohort study demonstrates the feasibility and initial effectiveness of a digitally supported shoulder rehabilitation system in routine clinical practice.
— BMC musculoskeletal disorders

4️⃣ [Neuro] Feasibility and safety of immersive virtual reality training device (ReMind) in post-stroke rehabilitation
This immersive virtual reality training device (ReMind) was found feasible and safe for post-stroke upper limb rehabilitation, with positive patient feedback.
— Bulletin of Faculty of Physical Therapy

5️⃣ [Neuro] Acceptability, Safety and Feasibility of a Sedentary Behaviour Intervention (REACH-SCI) in Individuals with Spinal Cord Injury: A Mixed-Methods Study
This mixed-methods study indicates that the REACH-SCI intervention, designed to reduce sedentary behavior in individuals with spinal cord injury, is acceptable, safe, and feasible, with preliminary efficacy in improving sedentary behavior, physical activity, and cardiovascular biomarkers.
