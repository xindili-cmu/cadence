Multiple RCTs Point to New Directions in Physical Therapy

15 updates today across 6 specialties. 5 worth your time 👇

1️⃣ [Orthopedic] Efficacy of Sensory Electrical Stimulation Versus Alternating Electromyography-Guided Electrical Stimulation on Functional Recovery of the Hand in Chronic Stroke: A Randomized Controlled Trial.
For hand function recovery in chronic stroke patients, both sensory electrical stimulation (SES) and alternating electromyography-guided electrical stimulation (Alt-EMG) are superior to conventional physical therapy alone, but there is no significant difference between the two.
— NeuroRehabilitation

2️⃣ [Orthopedic] Photobiomodulation therapy has a negligible effect as an adjunct to motor control exercise in people with chronic non-specific low back pain: a randomised trial.
This large RCT clearly shows that adding photobiomodulation therapy (PBMT) to motor control exercise offers no additional clinical benefit for chronic non-specific low back pain.
— Journal of physiotherapy

3️⃣ [Orthopedic] Six-month supervised telerehabilitation combining pilates-based therapeutic exercise and self-myofascial release in adults with fibromyalgia: a randomized controlled trial
							| Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine
This RCT found that a 6-month supervised telerehabilitation program combining Pilates and self-myofascial release significantly improved fibromyalgia impact, sleep quality, and psychological outcomes compared to usual care.
— Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine

4️⃣ [Neuro] Effects of Exercise Training on the Metabolic Cost of Walking in Health and Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis.
This meta-analysis clearly indicates that chronic exercise training improves the metabolic cost of walking in healthy adults, with smaller effects in stroke patients, and obesity may diminish this benefit.
— Sports medicine (Auckland, N.Z.)

5️⃣ [Geriatric] Association of dysphagia with physical performance and fall risk in frail older adults
This observational study found that dysphagia in frail older adults is significantly associated with poorer physical performance (lower SPPB scores) and increased fall risk, underscoring the importance of swallowing assessments in geriatric care.
— BMC Geriatrics
