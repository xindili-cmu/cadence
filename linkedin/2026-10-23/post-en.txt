RCTs and Observational Studies in Geriatric, Neurological, and Orthopedic Rehab

6 updates today across 4 specialties. 5 worth your time 👇

1️⃣ [Orthopedic] Hybrid versus in-clinic rehabilitation for non-traumatic shoulder pain within a standardized digital care pathway: a prospective longitudinal observational study
This observational study suggests that for non-traumatic shoulder pain, hybrid rehabilitation (one in-clinic, two home sessions weekly) shows no clear difference in time to clinical discharge and functional outcomes compared to in-clinic rehabilitation (three in-clinic sessions weekly).
— BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders

2️⃣ [Neuro] Effect of bimodal signal biofeedback on the supraglottic swallowing maneuver in patients with post-stroke dysphagia: A randomised controlled trial
This RCT introduces an innovative biofeedback approach for post-stroke dysphagia, combining an accelerometer and nasal airflow sensor to potentially enhance the supraglottic swallowing maneuver.
— Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation

3️⃣ [Geriatric] Elastic band resistance training improves physical function, gait, cardiac indices, and corticomuscular coherence in older women at risk of sarcopenia: a randomized controlled trial
For older women at risk of sarcopenia in the community, 12 weeks of elastic band resistance training significantly improves muscle strength, gait, and even shows positive effects on cardiac morphology and function.
— BMC Geriatrics

4️⃣ [Manual & Modality] Effectiveness of Kalevala Manual Therapy, Health Coaching, and Physiotherapy for Chronic Low Back Pain: A Randomized Controlled Trial (The INTEGRA Trial)
This RCT with 81 chronic low back pain patients tells us that if you're using Kalevala Manual Therapy (KMT), its effects on pain and disability are comparable to guideline-based physiotherapy.

5️⃣ [Manual & Modality] Diagnostic accuracy and discriminative validity of clinician-administered physiotherapy examination tests in adults with nonspecific neck pain: a systematic review
Current evidence suggests that clinician-administered physiotherapy examination tests can help characterize impairment patterns in adults with non-specific neck pain, particularly in sensorimotor, muscle performance, and movement control domains.
— BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders
