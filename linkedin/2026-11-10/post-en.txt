Evidence for TCM Exercises in MSK; Sleep Intervention on Athletic Performance

10 updates today across 5 specialties. 5 worth your time 👇

1️⃣ [Orthopedic] Can traditional Chinese exercises help people with musculoskeletal disorders? - BJSM blog - social media's leading SEM voice
Traditional Chinese exercises have high or moderate certainty evidence supporting their efficacy in improving physical function, mental health, and pain for various musculoskeletal disorders (e.g., knee OA, neck pain, fibromyalgia, osteoporosis, chronic ankle instability).
— British Journal of Sports Medicine

2️⃣ [Sports] Effects of sleep manipulation on athletic performance, cognitive function, and recovery: a systematic review of experimental studies
Sleep extension is generally associated with improved athletic performance, cognitive function, and recovery (e.g., 0.7s reduction in sprint time, 3% increase in endurance), while sleep restriction has adverse effects.
— BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation

3️⃣ [Neuro] Comparison of robot-assisted gait training and visual feedback balance training on balance and gait outcomes in patients with stroke: a randomized controlled trial.
For stroke patients' balance and gait rehabilitation, visual feedback balance training shows a slight advantage over robot-assisted gait training in improving Berg Balance Scale scores and reducing fear of falling.
— Physiotherapy theory and practice

4️⃣ [Sports] Comparison of the effects of modified razor curl and nordic hamstring exercises on hamstring muscle strength and posterior-chain endurance in male football players
Both modified razor curl (MRCE) and Nordic hamstring exercises (NHE) are effective in improving eccentric hamstring strength and trunk-hip extensor endurance in male football players.
— BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation

5️⃣ [Neuro] Effects of adjunctive craniosacral therapy to conventional physiotherapy on postural responses and autonomic function in individuals with multiple sclerosis: a randomized controlled trial.
Craniosacral Therapy, as an adjunct to conventional physiotherapy for multiple sclerosis patients, may influence specific postural response parameters, but its overall impact on balance and autonomic function remains unclear.
— Physiotherapy theory and practice
