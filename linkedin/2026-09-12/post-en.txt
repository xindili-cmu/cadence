Cost-effectiveness of non-pharmacologic TBI cognitive rehab in older adults; exercise snacking for frailty; dry needling for linear scar compliance

7 updates today across 6 specialties. 5 worth your time 👇

1️⃣ [Neuro] Cost-utility of non-pharmacologic interventions aimed at improving cognitive outcomes after traumatic brain injury in older adulthood from the Ontario public healthcare payer perspective
This cost-utility analysis clearly indicates that for older adults with traumatic brain injury (TBI) within Ontario, Canada's public healthcare system, combined physical rehabilitation and cognitive training is the most cost-effective intervention, significantly improving QALYs and reducing dementia incidence.
— BMC Geriatrics

2️⃣ [Geriatric] Exercise snacking as a novel strategy for prevention and intervention of frailty in older adults: a scoping review
This scoping review suggests that 'exercise snacking' is a viable new strategy for frailty prevention and intervention in older adults.
— BMC Geriatrics

3️⃣ [Manual & Modality] Scar dry needling for tissue compliance in individuals with linear scars: a randomized controlled trial.
Evidence for dry needling in scar management is limited, but this study suggests it can improve tissue compliance in linear scars short-term.
— The Journal of manual & manipulative therapy

4️⃣ [Pediatric] Centering First Nations parents' voices: a community-directed pediatric rehabilitation needs assessment in Northern Saskatchewan.
This community-directed study highlights severe systemic inequities in pediatric rehabilitation services for Indigenous children in Northern Saskatchewan, including lack of information, service delays, and insufficient resources.
— Disability and rehabilitation

5️⃣ [Pediatric] The effects of robot assisted therapy on upper limb functions and gait parameters in cerebral palsy: A randomized controlled trial.
This RCT suggests that adding upper extremity robot-assisted training to conventional physiotherapy for children with cerebral palsy can improve upper extremity function and positively influence gait parameters.
— Brazilian journal of physical therapy
