Plyometric training for female athletes and manual therapy for infantile torticollis: Evidence updates

11 updates today across 5 specialties. 5 worth your time 👇

1️⃣ [Sports] Female Team-Sport Athletes’ Physical Fitness and Jump Performance Kinematics After Plyometric Jump Training: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis
Be aware that plyometric jump training (PJT) significantly boosts countermovement jump height (ES = 0.550), drop jump reactive strength index (ES = 0.926), linear sprint speed, and agility sprint speed in adult female team-sport athletes.
— Sports Medicine - Open

2️⃣ [Pediatric] Efficacy of Manual Physiotherapy Techniques for Improving Cervical Range of Motion in Infants With Congenital Muscular Torticollis: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
This systematic review and meta-analysis (5 RCTs, 143 infants) strongly confirms that manual physiotherapy significantly improves cervical ROM in infants with congenital muscular torticollis (Cohen's d = 0.90).
— Physiotherapy research international : the journal for researchers and clinicians in physical therapy

3️⃣ [Orthopedic] Feasibility of an interprofessional rehabilitation program (IPRehab-LBP) for people with chronic low back pain in Ethiopia: an open-label pilot randomized controlled trial with an embedded process evaluation
This pilot RCT for chronic low back pain (CLBP) confirms the feasibility and acceptability of an interprofessional rehabilitation program (IPRehab-LBP) in Ethiopia.
— BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders

4️⃣ [Orthopedic] Feasibility, Safety, and Preliminary Effect Estimates of an Ultrasound-Informed Hybrid Joint-Sparing Rehabilitation Strategy in Patients With Rheumatoid Arthritis: A Prospective Non-Randomized Proof-of-Concept Study.
This proof-of-concept study shows an ultrasound-informed hybrid rehabilitation protocol is feasible, safe, and significantly reduces pain in RA patients.
— Physiotherapy research international : the journal for researchers and clinicians in physical therapy

5️⃣ [Neuro] Healthcare professionals' perceptions and experiences of promoting physical activity in persons with multiple sclerosis: a systematic review of qualitative evidence.
This systematic review highlights that effective physical activity promotion in MS patients requires targeted education, patient materials, referral pathways, and cross-sector coordination.
— Disability and rehabilitation
