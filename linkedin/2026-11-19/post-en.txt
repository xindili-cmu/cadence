Stroke Distal Upper Limb Recovery: Positive RCT Results for Transcranial Temporal Interference Stimulation

26 updates today across 8 specialties. 5 worth your time 👇

1️⃣ [Neuro] Selective distal upper extremity recovery following targeted dentate nucleus temporal interference stimulation in stroke-double-blind, sham-controlled randomized controlled trial with resting-state EEG
This high-quality RCT is the first to confirm that targeted dentate nucleus temporal interference stimulation (tTIS) combined with conventional rehabilitation significantly improves upper extremity motor function, especially distal control, in subacute stroke patients.
— Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation

2️⃣ [Neuro] Efficacy of action observation therapy in patients with dementia or cognitive impairment: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials.
Action observation therapy significantly improved cognitive function, ADL, and naming aphasia in patients with dementia or cognitive impairment.
— Disability and rehabilitation

3️⃣ [Sports] Comparative Effects of Different Neuromuscular Training Modalities and Doses on Lower-Limb Injury Prevention in Athletes: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis
This systematic review of 27,663 participants suggests that neuromuscular training (NMT) may reduce lower-limb injury risk (RR=0.72), but the certainty of evidence is very low.
— Sports Medicine - Open

4️⃣ [Pediatric] Body-weight support is the dominant contributor to elevated walking cost in cerebral palsy
This study found that body-weight support is the dominant contributor to elevated walking cost in children with cerebral palsy.
— Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation

5️⃣ [Orthopedic] Aerobic and aerobic–cognitive training in knee osteoarthritis following acute exercise intensity selection: a two-phase randomised controlled trial protocol
This trial protocol aims to optimize exercise intensity for knee osteoarthritis and explore the potential of cognitive training combined with aerobic exercise.
— BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation
