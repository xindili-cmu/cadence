Chronic Ankle Instability Management: RCT Evidence for NMES + BFRT and Subtalar Sling Taping

4 updates today across 4 specialties. 4 worth your time 👇

1️⃣ [Orthopedic] Effects of neuromuscular electrical stimulation combined with blood flow restriction training on lower-leg neuromuscular function in individuals with chronic ankle instability: a randomized controlled trial
For individuals with chronic ankle instability, combining neuromuscular electrical stimulation (NMES) and blood flow restriction training (BFRT) with a standardized exercise program may offer broader short-term benefits than exercise alone or isolated modalities, especially in peroneus longus activation and eversion strength.
— BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation

2️⃣ [Pediatric] Rotational perturbation training improves muscle coordination during reactive standing balance in trained and untrained conditions in children with spastic cerebral palsy.
This intervention study found that rotational perturbation training effectively improves reactive standing balance and muscle coordination in children with spastic cerebral palsy, and this improvement generalizes to untrained perturbation conditions.

3️⃣ [Manual & Modality] Immediate and fatigue-related effects of subtalar sling taping in individuals with chronic ankle instability.
For individuals with chronic ankle instability, both kinesiology tape and rigid taping can improve performance on the Y-Balance Test after fatigue, but effects vary by outcome and time point, with no consistent superiority of one over the other.
— Physical therapy in sport : official journal of the Association of Chartered Physiotherapists in Sports Medicine

4️⃣ [Geriatric] Autonomic responses to repeated locomotor perturbations in older adults: dissociation between phasic habituation and sustained tonic arousal.
Older adults exhibit sympathetic nervous system responses to balance perturbations during walking, rapidly habituating to repeated, same-direction perturbations, but showing renewed responses to novel perturbation directions.
— Journal of electromyography and kinesiology : official journal of the International Society of Electrophysiological Kinesiology
