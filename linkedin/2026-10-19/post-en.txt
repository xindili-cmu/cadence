Two RCTs for Chronic LBP: Inspiratory Muscle Training and Treadmill with Traction on Various Outcomes

7 updates today across 3 specialties. 5 worth your time 👇

1️⃣ [Orthopedic] The effect of functional inspiratory muscle training on diaphragm function and core stabilisation in patients with chronic non-specific low back pain: a randomised controlled trial
This RCT clearly indicates that Functional Inspiratory Muscle Training (F-IMT) is superior to thoracic expansion exercises for improving diaphragm function and core stabilization in patients with chronic non-specific low back pain (CNSLBP).
— BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders

2️⃣ [Orthopedic] Effects of treadmill exercise with and without vertical traction on gait, pain, disability, trunk proprioception, and depression in patients with chronic mechanical low back pain: A randomized controlled trial.
For patients with chronic low back pain, adding vertical traction and partial body weight support to treadmill exercise can further improve gait, reduce disability, and enhance trunk proprioception.
— Gait & posture

3️⃣ [Orthopedic] Barriers and Facilitators Influencing Physiotherapists' Adherence to Clinical Practice Guidelines and Return to Sport Criteria after Anterior Cruciate Ligament Reconstruction: A Qualitative Study in Austria.
Adherence to clinical practice guidelines (CPGs) in ACLR rehabilitation remains suboptimal.
— International journal of sports physical therapy

4️⃣ [Geriatric] Restoring Physical Resilience: Consistent Functional and Sarcopenia Gains Across Two Vivifrail Cohorts
This observational study on the Vivifrail model suggests that a 36-week community multicomponent exercise program significantly improves physical function in older adults (average 1.33 point increase in SPPB scores) and reduces probable sarcopenia prevalence (from 17.4% to 5.4%).

5️⃣ [Sports] Quality of Movement Assessment after ACL Reconstruction in Youth: A Clinical Framework to Guide Rehabilitation and Return-to-Sport Decision-Making.
In pediatric and adolescent ACLR rehab, you should consider incorporating quality of movement assessments into return-to-sport decisions.
— International journal of sports physical therapy
