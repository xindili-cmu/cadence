ESWT and Physical Activity Guidelines Show Supporting Evidence

8 updates today across 3 specialties. 5 worth your time 👇

1️⃣ [Orthopedic] Effects of extracorporeal shockwave therapy on pain intensity and functional disability in chronic low back pain: a systematic review and meta-analysis.
For chronic low back pain, extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT), especially radial ESWT (rESWT), shows a moderate effect size (SMD = -0.72) for short-term pain reduction, but functional improvements are not clinically meaningful.
— Disability and rehabilitation

2️⃣ [Neuro] Physical activity guidelines' impact on cognition in multiple sclerosis: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials.
This systematic review clearly indicates that meeting physical activity guidelines for people with multiple sclerosis (MS) can significantly improve processing speed and memory.
— Disability and rehabilitation

3️⃣ [Orthopedic] Clinical and functional outcomes of aquatic plyometrics in adolescent patellofemoral pain syndrome: a dual-arm randomized controlled trial
							| Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine
This RCT provides strong evidence that aquatic plyometrics are superior to conventional exercises for improving pain, strength, function, and quality of life in adolescents with patellofemoral pain syndrome.
— Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine

4️⃣ [Orthopedic] Feasibility of Distributed Wearable Ultrasound for Detecting Neuromuscular Coordination Differences After Knee Injury
Wearable ultrasound systems show feasibility in detecting neuromuscular coordination differences after knee injury, revealing issues not reflected by traditional force plate biomechanics.

5️⃣ [Geriatric] Low balance confidence in relation to strength training, walking, sitting, and physical function among women aged 75–105 years: a cross-sectional study within the Women’s Health Initiative Strong and Healthy (WHISH) Intervention Cohort
This cross-sectional study in older women (75-105 years) identifies strong associations between lower balance confidence and factors like sitting time, while higher confidence is linked to strength training, walking, and better physical function.
— BMC Geriatrics
