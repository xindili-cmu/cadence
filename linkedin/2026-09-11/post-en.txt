Rotator Cuff VR Training, Stroke Cortical Activation, Sarcopenia Assessment: 3 High-Score Studies

6 updates today across 4 specialties. 5 worth your time 👇

1️⃣ [Orthopedic] Effects of Virtual Reality–Assisted Task-Oriented Training on Clinical and Psychosocial Outcomes in Individuals with Rotator Cuff Tears: A Randomized Controlled Single-Blind Trial
This preliminary RCT suggests that adding VR-assisted task-oriented training to conventional physical therapy may positively impact clinical and psychosocial outcomes for rotator cuff tear patients.
— Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation

2️⃣ [Neuro] Load-dependent Motor Cortical Activation Assessed by fNIRS Is Partially Preserved After Stroke.
This study suggests that the posterior parietal cortex (PPC) in stroke patients may play a compensatory role in load-dependent motor tasks, maintaining load-proportional activation distinct from primary motor areas.
— American journal of physical medicine & rehabilitation

3️⃣ [Geriatric] Sarcopenia Assessment in Resource-Constrained Settings: Expert Agreement on Surrogate Measures
For sarcopenia assessment in resource-constrained settings, the 5-times sit-to-stand test (5STS), Timed Up and Go (TUG), and calf circumference (CC) are highly endorsed by experts as surrogate measures.

4️⃣ [Geriatric] C-reactive protein-to-albumin ratio adds independent prognostic value to the clinical frailty scale in older emergency department patients: a multicenter cohort study
The C-reactive protein-to-albumin ratio (CAR), combined with the Clinical Frailty Scale (CFS), provides independent prognostic value in older emergency department patients.
— BMC Geriatrics

5️⃣ [Neuro] Errorless learning of a video call in Alzheimer Disease: verbal motor error dissociation as a potential marker of procedural learning (a pilot study)
This pilot study suggests that errorless learning can help individuals with Alzheimer's disease acquire new daily communication tasks, supporting the use of preserved procedural memory in rehabilitation.
